## Measured (11-28 14:2x CST, from the code)
- `GET /api/connections/held` (server.js ~19130) answers `{ services: connectionsHeld() }` (server.js ~1956): one row per service, `{ name, route, how: 'sign-in' | 'token', held, connect?, tool? }`. GitHub (a device sign-in, else its tool's own sign-in), Vercel (its tool's own sign-in), Cloudflare (a token file), then every token door from `tokendoors.routes()` (the search services and the rest).
- `held` is `true` (Kosmos has it on disk, or the tool says it is signed in), `false` (nothing saved), or `null` (a tool sign-in Kosmos cannot see from disk). It reads the disk only, never a verify(), so it costs nothing and bills nobody.
- `GET /api/connections`, the page's read, runs a live verify() on every door, and the search services bill the person's own quota for each (#1636). The phone never calls it.

## The words (D2b1), the weakest part
`held` is the disk's record, not a live check, so the line must not claim more than that:
- `true`: "Saved on your computer" (a token) / "Signed in on your computer" (a sign-in);
- `false`: "Not connected";
- `null`: "Signed in through its own tool, if at all: Kosmos cannot tell from here".
These are mine, not the page's (the page words a live verify's answer, which the phone does not have). Weakest premise: a revoked key still reads "Saved on your computer". The line says "saved", not "working", for that reason. What would change my mind: Josh wanting the live state on the phone, which would mean one explicit tap that calls the sweep, never a read on open.
