# w4-4301: why win32apply W4 failed once on windows-latest, and removing the risk (#4301)

## Root cause
- These tests run the product on sim.deps(), whose sleep and sleepSync only move a fake clock. The
  real time goes to what the sim does not replace:
  - the held renames win32swap.renameWithRetry really waits through, about 1 s per try, so about 6 s
    for a journal write held past OWNER_READ_BUDGET's tries;
  - the real shims a test spawns.
  W4 does two held-past-budget rollbacks, a hold of a window and a half, and spawns shims. Its floor
  is about 15 s before any slowness, so 30 s left under 2x headroom. The failing attempt ran the file
  about 40% slower than its re-run.
- A node:test timeout is a timer, so it can fire only while the test waits on real async work. Here
  that means awaiting a real process (bootShimAsync). A test that never does cannot be cut off by its
  limit: PROBE6-E ran 36 s on T and passed. So the tests at risk are the ones that await a real
  process, not simply the slow ones.
- The failing attempt's own log agrees. The old filter printed every line containing "Error", and
  under W4 it printed nothing. An assertion failure would have printed "AssertionError"; a timeout's
  "test timed out after 30000ms" contains no "Error".

## Build
- tools/windows-tests.js: failureDetail prints node's "failing tests:" section, one capped entry per
  failing test, instead of lines containing "Error".
  - Tests not expected to fail print first, and it says what it left out (lines per test, and whole
    tests past ten).
  - Only real stack frames are dropped.
  - A crash before the section falls back to the output's tail.
  - Tested in engine/windows-tests-1777.test.js.
- engine/win32apply.test.js: a HELD profile (120 s). The rule: every test that awaits a real
  process is HELD, since only those can be cut off by their limit, and a stall of about 34 s can take
  any of them past 30 s. That is W4, W5, P2, P3, P4 and P8. HELD covers the worst of them plus that
  stall (W4, 28.0 s + 34 s, about 62 s) about twice over. H1 spawns nothing real and stays on T;
  BUG 1 is already on WINDOWS_ONLY (120 s) and P5b on LONG.

## Decided
- 120 s: the worst process-awaiting test plus the largest stall seen, about twice over, and the
  file's own budget for its held-handle tests. An earlier iteration had 150 s from a 4.5x scaling
  model; the unfiltered probe refuted that model, and 120 s is below the raise Liu Kang approved.
- Rejected:
  - Raising T for the whole file: it widens 74 tests that do not need it.
  - Moving W4 and W5 to LONG: a hang would then take 10 minutes to show.
  - Shrinking the product budgets inside the test: that would stop testing the real budgets, which is
    what W4 is for.

## Weakest part
- The one failure's actual message was not captured, because the log fix came after. The cause is
  inferred from several pieces: W4 measured 93% of its budget on a quiet runner; the failing run was
  about 40% slower; the old filter's empty output is the signature a timeout leaves; and the control
  run shows a timeout is what cuts W4 off.
- The stall size (about 34 s) comes from two days of probes on one runner image; a longer stall would
  still fit inside 120 s for every HELD test up to about 90 s.
- A stall is a property of the runner, not of this file. In other files, a test that awaits real
  work on a 30 s budget, or under the windows job's 60 s per-test default, can be cut off the same
  way. That is beyond this card; the windows job's failure detail now says so when it happens.
- Nothing keeps a NEW process-awaiting test that is slow off T; the rule is written where the next
  author of one will see it (beside HELD).
