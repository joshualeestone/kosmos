# devicedoor-4656: the #4326 order test must not have a speed budget (kosmos#4656)

## Done when
- engine/devicedoor-bounded-4326.test.js's order and control arms cannot fail because the box is slow.
- The order arm still proves what it guards: the answer is stdout THEN stderr whatever order they arrive in, and fails against a runBounded that answers in arrival order.
- The arms whose claim IS time keep a time claim, but one a slow box cannot miss: the clock starts once the child is spawned, and the flood and spawn-failure arms claim "well before the timeout" (a third of 30 s) rather than a fixed 5 s or 2 s. The SIGTERM arm keeps its 350 to 2000 ms window, now measured from the spawn.
- Test-only: no product code changes.

## What changed (engine/devicedoor-bounded-4326.test.js only)
1. NOT_A_SPEED_TEST = 30000 ms budget for the control arm (both scripts) and the order arm, in place of 5000. A 0.2 s script against 30 s is 150x headroom; the card's failure was a 5 s budget met at load 9.7 on 10 cores during a disk-full window.
2. The order arm now checks both arrival orders: stderr then stdout (the case that matters), and stdout then stderr.
3. It no longer assumes the 0.2 s sleep made stderr arrive first: the test listens to the child's own streams (runBounded returns the child before any data arrives) and repeats the stderr-first script, at most 5 times, until stderr was SEEN to arrive first. A PREMISE assertion fails if that never happened, so a slow box cannot turn the arm vacuous.

## Rejected
- No sleep at all: then which stream arrives first is up to the scheduler, and the arm could pass without ever exercising the reordering. The seen-first loop keeps the sleep as a strong hint and checks the premise instead of trusting it.
- Raising only the timeout: fixes the flake, but keeps an unchecked premise (that stderr arrives first).

## Measured
- 6 of 6 tests pass.
- Control: a runBounded mutated to answer in arrival order fails the order arm ("stdout must come before stderr in the answer (stderr arrived first)"). The flood arm also fails under that mutant, only because the mutant's concatenation is uncapped. devicedoor.js restored byte for byte.
- 20 of 20 consecutive runs of the file green at 1-minute load 6.3 to 8.5 on 12 cores (other agents' suites running).

## Weakest part
Nothing on the box recorded spawn latency at the minute of the original failure, so the exact cause of a 5 s spawn stays unmeasured; the fix does not depend on it.

## Review 1 (opus), fixed
- The stdout-first half had no seen-first check: an answer that puts the LAST stream to arrive first passed the stderr-first half and was caught by the stdout-first one only when stdout really came first. Both halves now go through one helper (answerWhenFirst) with the same up-to-5 loop and PREMISE assertion.
- The time-claim arms counted spawn time, the likely thing a saturated box slows: `run` now starts its clock after runBounded returns (a spawn that fails outright answers inside it, so there the clock never restarts); the flood and spawn-failure arms assert under a third of a 30 s timeout.
- The failure message names the first stream seen, or undefined when none was.
- Measured again: 6 of 6; mutants red: arrival order ("first to arrive: stderr"), last-arrived first ("first to arrive: stdout", the stdout-first half), a flood that is never stopped ("30001 ms"); devicedoor.js restored byte for byte. 20 of 20 consecutive runs at load 6.2 to 7.0.
