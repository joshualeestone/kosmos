# orgsector-4434: org chart branches in their own sectors, so connector lines never cross

Card: kosmos#4434 (Josh, #admin 2026-09-28 17:14 CDT; assigned by Liu Kang). On first load a manager's reports
sat outside the manager's angular sector, so their lines crossed another manager's.

## Finished means
On first load, and after any agent is added, removed or re-parented, every report sits inside its manager's
sector and no connector line crosses another at rest; faces keep their no-overlap spacing; the same input gives
the same positions. Pinned by web.org-sectors-4434.test.js (red on main) and the browser check
render-org-sectors-4434 (red on main, green here), which keeps a screenshot.

## Cause (main 41e887303)
`orgPlace` fanned every manager's reports over a FIXED 69 degree arc centred on the manager, whatever room it
had, so neighbouring fans overlapped; the physics had no notion of branches.

## Design (current)
- A flat fleet (nobody reports to anybody) keeps main's layout and physics exactly.
- A tree is laid out by sectors: each first-ring agent owns a slice sized by its leaf count and sits at its
  centre; a manager's reports share a window centred on it, split by leaf count. For a manager that has a
  manager, the window is capped at the tangent angle (acos(r_manager / r_report) * 0.9), because only then can
  a report's line dip across its grandparent's line; a first-ring manager's reports stay within ~85 degrees,
  so no line sweeps over the hub. Each ring grows (at least 8px and 3% a try) until no two nodes are closer
  than ORG_MIN_ARC.
- Physics for a tree: each node is pulled back to its placed position relative to the hub (ORG_SIM.home
  0.08), and its parent spring rests at the placed length.
- orgPlanarRepair: when a tree comes to REST (orgLiveSettle; the animation's stop, never mid-drag) with two
  lines crossing, or two lines from one manager lying along each other, the hub returns home and every node to
  its placed position. It never moves a held node or hub.
- A tree whose every node already sits at its placement (first load, and every repaint after a rest the
  repair ended) is painted there and the physics does not run: on a large or squeezed tree the physics drifts
  it into a crossing and the repair then snaps it back. A drag, a re-parent or a new agent starts it off its
  placement, and those still move (and still end with the repair). A flat fleet is untouched.
- The repair's crossing test, like the tests' count, ignores rounding: two radial lines at exactly opposite
  angles lie on one line through the hub and never meet, but came out as a "crossing" of tiny mixed signs.
- orgFit never squeezes a tree below the scale that keeps its faces 46px apart (orgFloorOf: kMin = 46 /
  closest placed pair, called by paintOrg and by the tests); a tree that needs more room scrolls.
- In the animation the repair GLIDES home (orgHomeStep: the whole chart the same fraction, at most 40px a
  frame, landing exactly on the placement); the reduced-motion settle snaps, since it does not animate. The
  repair also fires on two faces closer than 44px at rest. A click (a press that never moved) leaves a tree
  where it rests.

## Decisions (mine, reversible)
- Leaf-count weighting; the flat path untouched.
- Charts grow for a lead with ten or more reports: a first-ring manager's reports share one ring within ~85
  degrees either side of it, and the ring grows until they are 62px apart. Measured (review it5, natural
  size, main then branch): lead + 15 544 -> 791px; + 30 660 -> 1410 (1009 at 375px); + 50 776 -> 2290 (1626
  at 375px); + 300 2284 -> 12732. Random trees, median: 60 agents 1136 -> 1484, 100 agents 1284 -> 2198.
  That is the cost of no crossings and no overlaps (main fitted smaller by overlapping faces). DECIDED: ship
  it, state it in the PR for Josh, and file a follow-up card for lanes (a big team's leaf reports on two or
  three staggered rings: lines from one manager cannot cross each other, so lanes are crossing-safe), which
  would bring a big team back near main's size. Weakest part: I have not built lanes, so "near main's size"
  is an estimate. What would change my mind: Josh saying a lead with 30+ reports is common on his fleets
  now, which would make lanes part of this card.
- A stronger home pull was rejected (it traded crossings for overlapping faces); the rest-time repair and the
  squeeze floor close the squeezed case instead.
- Dragging is a gesture: lines may cross while a person holds a node; the chart is repaired when it rests.
- render-org-reduced-motion's board is now thirty flat agents at 375px: a tree at its placement is not
  settled at all, so the old tree board passed with the #1738 settle deleted. Flat, it is red with the settle
  reverted (12 pairs, 43px) and green with it (51px); DENSE_MAX back to 64.
- Known, not fixed here (review it5 NIT): on trees of 80-300 agents a hub line can pass close to an unrelated
  first-ring face at almost the same angle (main: 36,342 such cases against 325 in the same family).
