# orgsector-4434: org chart branches in their own sectors, so connector lines never cross

Card: kosmos#4434 (Josh, #admin 2026-09-28 17:14 CDT; assigned by Liu Kang). On first load a manager's reports
sat outside the manager's angular sector, so their lines crossed another manager's.

## Finished means
On first load, and after any agent is added, removed or re-parented, every report sits inside its manager's
sector and no connector line crosses another at rest; faces keep their no-overlap spacing; the same input gives
the same positions. Pinned by web.org-sectors-4434.test.js (red on main) and the browser check
render-org-sectors-4434 (red on main, green here), which keeps a screenshot.

## Cause (main 41e887303)
`orgPlace` fanned every manager's reports over a FIXED 69 degree arc centred on the manager, whatever room it
had, so neighbouring fans overlapped; the physics had no notion of branches.

## Design (current)
- A flat fleet (nobody reports to anybody) keeps main's layout and physics exactly.
- A tree is laid out by sectors: each first-ring agent owns a slice sized by its leaf count and sits at its
  centre; a manager's reports share a window centred on it, split by leaf count. For a manager that has a
  manager, the window is capped at the tangent angle (acos(r_manager / r_report) * 0.9), because only then can
  a report's line dip across its grandparent's line; a first-ring manager's reports stay within ~85 degrees,
  so no line sweeps over the hub. Each ring grows (at least 8px and 3% a try) until no two nodes are closer
  than ORG_MIN_ARC.
- Physics for a tree: each node is pulled back to its placed position relative to the hub (ORG_SIM.home
  0.08), and its parent spring rests at the placed length.
- orgPlanarRepair: when a tree comes to REST (orgLiveSettle; the animation's stop, never mid-drag) with two
  lines crossing, or two lines from one manager lying along each other, the hub returns home and every node to
  its placed position. It never moves a held node or hub.
- A tree whose every node already sits at its placement (first load, and every repaint after a rest the
  repair ended) is painted there and the physics does not run: on a large or squeezed tree the physics drifts
  it into a crossing and the repair then snaps it back. A drag, a re-parent or a new agent starts it off its
  placement, and those still move (and still end with the repair). A flat fleet is untouched.
- The repair's crossing test, like the tests' count, ignores rounding: two radial lines at exactly opposite
  angles lie on one line through the hub and never meet, but came out as a "crossing" of tiny mixed signs.
- orgFit never squeezes a tree below the scale that keeps its faces 46px apart (kMin = 46 / closest placed
  pair); a tree that needs more room scrolls.

## Decisions (mine, reversible)
- Leaf-count weighting; the flat path untouched.
- Charts grow for a lead with ten or more reports (measured by review it3: lead + 20 goes from 660 to 1010px
  natural, 437 to 682px at 375px). That is the cost of both no crossings and no overlaps: main fitted smaller
  by overlapping the faces and letting the physics push them apart.
- A stronger home pull was rejected (it traded crossings for overlapping faces); the rest-time repair and the
  squeeze floor close the squeezed case instead.
- Dragging is a gesture: lines may cross while a person holds a node; the chart is repaired when it rests.

## Measured (2026-09-28)
- web.org-sectors-4434.test.js on main: red (first paint, settle, re-parent). On this branch 20 tests pass, and
  web.org-view + org-reduced-motion-settle-1738 stay green (51 across the three, measured with --test-reporter=tap).
- Mutations, each turning its own test red: no tangent cap (randomTree(892, 85, 0.1)), no root limit
  (hub clearance), even weights, no home pull, no placed rest, no repair, no held guard, no hub reset, linear
  ring growth, no squeeze floor (overlaps at 375px), the at-placement rule off, no rounding margin (in the
  page's repair or in the test's count), the repair's along-each-other branch off. The 0.9 margin is
  unarmable by design: 0.9, 0.95 and 1.0 all give 0 crossings over 4,500 trees with a rounding-aware count.
- At-placement rule (scratchpad os-loop big.js): before it, a settle from the placement fired the repair in
  0/60 trees of 60-100 agents at full size but 3, 10 and 27 of 60 at 100-150, 150-220 and 220-300 (drift up
  to 27px, the snap up to ~150px with the hub, review it4); squeezed, 18 to 51 of 60. With it, the first load
  does not move. Drag release (os-it3 release.js) is unchanged from before: the physics is not touched.
- Rejected physics changes (measured, os-loop and os-it3 floor.js): no long-range push for trees (first load
  clean, but a released node stalls against the hub: 20/80 releases snapped up to 342px at full size, 11/80
  with a minimum-energy floor); subtracting each node's push at its placement (first load clean, but near the
  placement that correction outweighs the pull home: 22/80 releases snapped at 375px, 31 overlaps at rest).
- Repaint loop (scratchpad os-loop, the it3 harness plus the squeeze floor): with the floor the repair fires in
  2 to 3 of 150 fleets of 60 to 100 agents at 375, 1000 and 1440px, with no overlaps after it; but 40 of 1200
  (seed, width) cases fired on every one of four repaints, snapping about 130px each time. The layout memory
  closed it first; the at-placement rule replaced the memory (it covers this case: the repair ends every node
  exactly at its placement).
- Review harnesses (scratchpad os-rw, os-it3): settled crossings 0 up to 60 agents at 0/375/1000px before the
  repair; the repair and floor close the 60-90 squeezed cases; flat fleets bit-identical to main; phone fit
  for a lead with 3-8 reports identical to main.
- Browser: render-org-sectors-4434 on its own board (P17, fifteen agents, five managers, a tree that crosses on
  main even after settling): RED on main (n4 x n5, both modes), GREEN here. render-org-reduced-motion: its
  premise (the static layout overlaps) is gone by design; the dense board settles at 66px, DENSE_MAX 64 -> 72.

## History
- The first design clamped nodes to their sectors in the physics; review it1 showed trees still crossed after
  settling (the spring rest fought the grown rings) and released nodes jumped. I had written "the sector clamp
  alone holds over 300 settled trees": that was a too-narrow sample, and false.
- Review it2: squeezed large fleets still crossed at rest (then the repair). Review it3: the repair could run
  mid-drag, and squeezed placements overlapped (then the held guard and the squeeze floor).
- Review it4: no blocker. The 0.9 comment looked false because 1.0 crossed randomTree(1953, 39), but that
  crossing, and every crossing that pinned the cap, was rounding on two exactly opposite radial lines; my
  4,500-tree sweep first reported 3 "crossings" at 0.9 for the same reason. The counts now ignore rounding and
  the cap is pinned by a tree that really crosses. The along-each-other branch had no test; it has one. Large
  trees snapped on first load; they are no longer animated at their placement.
