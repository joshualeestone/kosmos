# zwnj-7892: a zero-width joiner or non-joiner inside a Persian or Indic word is spelling (kosmos#7892)

A leftover carried to #7892 from #7909's review: `shownText` escaped and marked every ZWNJ (U+200C) and ZWJ
(U+200D), so ordinary Persian and Indic names read as unusual, and a flag that fires on ordinary names stops being
read.

## Change (engine/seadmin.js)
`joinerInWord`: a ZWNJ or ZWJ is kept as it is, and does not mark the text, only between two letters of the same
Arabic-family or Indic script (Arabic, Syriac, Devanagari, Bengali, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Oriya, Tamil, Telugu,
Kannada, Malayalam, Sinhala), skipping marks (a virama) before it. Anywhere else it is escaped and marked as before.
The comment's known-false-positive list loses the Persian/Indic entry; the source carries no raw joiner (examples are
written as escapes).

## Tests (engine/seadmin-7116.test.js)
Persian "mi" + ZWNJ + "khaham" and Hindi ka + virama + ZWJ + ssa stay as they are and unmarked; a Latin name, two
scripts, either end of a word, and Cyrillic letters around a ZWJ are still escaped and marked. Three mutants (no rule,
any script, no virama skip) turn it red. 20/20, engine.reachable 8/8.

## Weakest premise
That a same-script joiner cannot be used to fake a name. It can still separate two visually identical spellings
("a" + ZWNJ + "b" vs "ab" in Persian look different only in joining), which is the shape the rule now lets through
unmarked. The cost of marking every Persian name was judged higher; the fingerprint stays the defence for admins.
