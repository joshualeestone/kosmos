# korb-7896: the loading mark is the KOSMOS orb (kosmos#7896, slice 1: the app page)

Finished means: every `.spin.spin-sweep` in web/index.html (the Mac and Windows app) shows the KOSMOS orb Josh chose
on 2026-11-03, at the same size it has today (14 to 18 px), drawn and turning, its old dots hidden, held still for
reduced motion, and cheap enough for several on one screen.

Josh, 11-03 18:40 and 18:40 CST: "Let's do it. Can you replace it wherever we have the current spinning one?" and
"Would be ideal to keep it the same sized circle." 18:43: wants it in the mobile apps too (slice 4, its own branch).

Decided:
- Keep the markup. Every site keeps `<span class="spin spin-sweep" style="--sz:..">` with its eight `<i>`; KORB puts a
  canvas in each and the CSS hides the dots once it is there. So the size (--sz), the ink colour on a gold button
  (--spin-dot) and every existing markup check stay as they are, and a page whose script has not run still shows dots.
  Rejected: rewriting ten sites and seven checks to new markup, for no change the person can see.
- The small orb is not the big prototype scaled down. Measured on renders at 16, 24 and 48 px: 420 large dots made a
  blob with no letter; 1400 surface-heavy dots read K and O but not S or M; 2200 dots spread more evenly through the
  ball, each under 2% of the orb, read all four letters at 16 px.
- One animation loop for every orb, 30 frames a second (the turn is slow), drawing only visible ones, stopping when
  none is on the page; a MutationObserver starts it when one appears. Measured: about 2.6 ms per 16 px orb per frame
  in a software-rendered headless browser under heavy load.
- Reduced motion: drawn once, still, with the K facing (the Sweep calmed to a pulse; a still orb still reads as the mark).

Weakest premise: the cost on an older Windows laptop, not measured here. The 30 fps cap and the stop-when-none rule
bound it; if it shows, the next lever is fewer dots below 20 px.

(the record of Josh's pick needs the 11-03 banner) and allow-mock.html, and the iPhone app (Apple's ProgressView today).
