## Findings

- [WARNING] ios/Kosmos/ComposerExtras.swift:30, :157, :263; ios/Kosmos/ChatView.swift:1107; ios/Kosmos/RoomView.swift:1007. A second press on the microphone loses the very words that finishing is there to keep. Here is how. The person presses stop. `finish()` sets `finishing`, so `busy` stays true, and the button keeps exactly the listening look: the red `stop.circle.fill`, the label "Stop dictating", Send still dimmed. That lasts until the final result arrives, up to 2 s. The press looks as if it did nothing, so the natural thing is to press again. With VoiceOver, Send's hint "Stop dictating first" tells the person outright to stop again, even though dictation has already stopped. The second press goes to `toggle`, where `if starting || finishing { stop(); return }` calls `stop()`. That cancels the task and clears `live`, so the final result, which holds the last words and the punctuation, is thrown away. The rule "a stop by hand lets the last words land" is broken by the most ordinary follow-up. Fix: give finishing a look of its own (for example a small spinner, or the grey mic disabled, with the label "Finishing dictation"). During finishing, ignore a press or treat it as "keep waiting" instead of `stop()`. Change Send's hint during finishing to something like "Waiting for the last words". The 2 s cap already bounds the wait.

- [NIT] ios/Kosmos/ComposerExtras.swift:193, :227. `recognizer` is a local in `start()`, and nothing keeps it after `start()` returns. Only the task (`self.task`) is kept. Apple's SpokenWord sample keeps the `SFSpeechRecognizer` as a stored property for the whole session. Nothing documented says the task keeps its recognizer alive. If it does not, the task ends or never calls back, and the person sees dictation stop right after it starts, or never write anything. Confirm on a device, or simply store it (`private var recognizer: SFSpeechRecognizer?`, set in `start()` and cleared in `stop()`).

- [NIT] ios/Kosmos/ComposerExtras.swift:146-147, :281-290. When the person leaves the screen, the app goes to the background, a call starts, or headphones are plugged in or out, `stop()` runs, and it calls `task.cancel()`. Audio that was already captured but not yet transcribed (about the last half second of speech) is discarded. `request.endAudio()` with a short wait, as `finish()` does, would let it land. This matters most for an interruption or backgrounding: the microphone is gone, but the recognizer still holds audio it could finish. Not a rule violation as written, but it is a small loss of words.

- [NIT] ios/Kosmos/ComposerExtras.swift:149. While finishing, `engine.isRunning` is false because `finish()` stopped the engine, and `live` is still true. So any `AVAudioEngineConfigurationChange` that arrives in that window (a route change while the last words are on their way) runs `stop(say: true)` and drops the final result. Fix: also require `!finishing`, or `state == .listening`.

- [NIT] ios/Kosmos/ComposerExtras.swift:77, :147. After `mediaServicesWereResetNotification`, Apple's AVAudioSession documentation says to rebuild the app's audio objects. `engine` is a `let` and is never rebuilt. So every later press in the same composer may reach `engine.start()` on a dead engine and say "Dictation is not available right now. Try again in a moment." until the person leaves the conversation (a new `Dictation` comes with the view). This is rare. Fix: make the engine a `var` and replace it in the `.mediaReset` branch, re-registering the engine-change observer on the new engine.

- [NIT] ios/Kosmos/ComposerExtras.swift:134-139. The route filter treats any `.newDeviceAvailable` or `.oldDeviceUnavailable` as "headphones". With category `.record` and no `.allowBluetooth`, the microphone in use is the built-in one. If iOS posts these reasons for a Bluetooth device that is not in the recording route (AirPods connecting, or ear detection), dictation would stop although its microphone did not change. Confirm on a device. If it does happen, compare `previousRoute.inputs` with `currentRoute.inputs` and stop only when the input port changed.

- [NIT] ios/Kosmos/ChatView.swift:946, ios/Kosmos/RoomView.swift:1010; ComposerExtras.swift:310-314. The `.denied` line ("You can allow both in Settings, Kosmos.") stays under the box after the person goes to Settings, allows access and comes back. It is cleared only by the next press or by leaving the screen. Clearing a refusal state on `UIApplication.didBecomeActiveNotification` would stop it reading as still refused.

- [NIT] ios/LogicTests/composer/main.swift:57-86. The Dictation state machine has no behavioural test, only source-text pins. "A press while finishing stops", "no refusal after a hand stop" and "no refusal for 1110" are not pinned at all. The check at :71 only asks whether `dictation.reset(); emojiOpen = false }` appears anywhere in the file, not whether it sits inside `.onDisappear`, so moving it into another closure still passes. Each check can fail, so none is vacuous, but they guard wording, not behaviour. Consider extracting the result handler's decision (write, stop, refuse, say) into a pure function in ComposerLogic.swift and testing it there.
